Every eye color, explained. Brown eyes. Here's the thing: brown is the most common eye color on Earth. About seven out of ten people have it. In some parts of Africa and Asia, it's almost everyone. And the reason is one word: melanin. The front layer of a brown iris is packed with this dark pigment. It soaks up light instead of bouncing it back – that's why the eye looks brown. The more melanin, the darker the eye. Some brown eyes have so much, they almost look black. Truly black eyes don't exist – they're just very, very dark brown. But here's the part most people never learn: deep inside, every eye has a dark brown layer – even blue ones. Blue eyes simply have no pigment in front of it. And melanin isn't just color. It works like built-in sunglasses, soaking up strong light before it gets deeper into the eye. That's one reason brown eyes are most common in sunny parts of the world. And it gets even better. For most of human history, every single person on Earth had brown eyes. Blue only showed up around ten thousand years ago. So if your eyes are brown, you have the original human eye color.
