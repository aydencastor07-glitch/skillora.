Every mark on your skin, explained. Freckles. You were born with perfectly clear skin. Not a single freckle. Then, around age two, you spend your first summer in the sun – and tiny dots start to appear. Here's why. Deep in your skin live special cells called melanocytes. Their job: make melanin, a dark pigment that works like a natural sunscreen. When sunlight hits, they spread it out to protect you. In most people, they do it evenly. That's a tan. But in some spots, a small group of these cells works way harder than its neighbors. One busy little group, one freckle. And the reason is written in your DNA: a gene called MC1R. Certain versions of it make your skin bad at tanning – and great at freckling. That's why so many redheads are covered in them. In summer, your freckles go into overdrive and get darker. In winter, they fade and can almost disappear. But not every brown spot is a freckle. Sun spots show up after years of sun, and they stay all year. So if a spot suddenly changes, get it checked.
