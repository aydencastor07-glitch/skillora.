Every hair on your head, explained. Red hair. Only one or two out of every hundred people are born with it. Here's why. Remember the tiny workers in your hair root? They can make two colors: a dark pigment, and a red one. A gene called MC1R is their switch. When the switch works, they make dark color. When it's broken, they make red. And here's the crazy part: two parents with dark hair can both carry a broken switch without knowing it. Then their baby has a one in four chance of being a redhead. That same switch also changes how the body feels. In a 2004 study, redheads needed about 20 percent more anesthesia. Scientists think their pale skin helps them make vitamin D in cloudy places like Scotland and Ireland, where more than 1 in 10 people have red hair. But that skin burns fast, so sunscreen is not optional. And no, redheads are not going extinct. The gene just hides. So, do you have a redhead in your family?
